TravelTails — Your Travel Memories, Shared by Friends




What and why?

TravelTails is a travel companion app that helps people save and share their travel experiences through a virtual pet. Users can create a personal pet that collects memories and useful information from the places they visit, including photos, restaurants, attractions, recommendations, and personal advice.

The problem TravelTails aims to solve is that useful travel advice is often scattered across text messages, social media posts, notes, and conversations. When someone is planning a trip to a place that a friend has already visited, it can be difficult to find all of that friend's recommendations in one place. TravelTails provides a more personal way to preserve travel memories while also making them useful to friends who may visit the same destinations in the future.

The app combines a user's personal travel memories with recommendations from people they know. Instead of relying only on generic travel guides, users can receive information from friends whose experiences and preferences they already trust.

For whom?

The initial end users will be college students and young adults who travel, study abroad, or participate in study away programs. This includes our own friends, classmates, and other students who have traveled domestically or internationally.

Study away students are a particularly important group because they are often moving to an unfamiliar city or country and need practical recommendations from people who have already experienced the destination. They may want advice about restaurants, activities, transportation, local services, and places to visit. TravelTails would allow students who have previously studied away in a location to share their experiences and recommendations with students who are preparing to go there.

The app can also be useful for students returning from trips who want to preserve their memories and share their recommendations with friends. Our initial users can therefore be people within our own college community, making it possible to gather feedback from real students throughout the development process.

How?

From the user's perspective, TravelTails will allow users to:

Create and customize a virtual travel pet.
Add destinations they have visited to their pet's collection.
Save photos, restaurants, attractions, and other information from each trip.
Add personal recommendations and advice about destinations.
Organize their travel information by country or destination.
Connect with friends who also use the app.
Share their travel pet or specific destination information with friends.
Receive a friend's pet when traveling to a destination they have previously visited.
Explore the recommendations and memories that their friends have saved for that destination.
Add useful recommendations from friends to their own travel collection.
Continue updating their travel pet as they visit new places.

For example, if a student is preparing to study away in Japan, they could receive a friend's travel pet containing that friend's photos, favorite restaurants, attractions, and advice from their previous experience in Japan. The student could then use that information while preparing for their trip and save useful recommendations for later.

The goal is for the pet to become a personal travel companion that grows as the user travels and becomes a collection of memories and advice that can be shared with people they know.

Scope

TravelTails is intended to be challenging enough for a team of approximately 4–6 programmers while remaining achievable within one semester. The core system would require user accounts, profiles, friend connections, destination organization, photo and recommendation management, sharing between users, and a way to view and interact with a friend's travel information.

The project can be developed incrementally, beginning with the core functionality of creating a travel pet and saving destination information. Additional features such as friend sharing, pet customization, notifications, and improved destination organization can then be added as time permits.

The scope is large enough to provide meaningful software engineering challenges for a team while allowing the team to prioritize a functional minimum viable product if time or development resources become limited.
